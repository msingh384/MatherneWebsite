Mistakes That Cost the Most

What we see in almost
every new client engagement

These aren't edge cases. They're the situations that compound quietly for years — and that become very expensive the moment someone looks closely.

Filing FEIE when FTC was better

The Foreign Earned Income Exclusion and Foreign Tax Credit are not interchangeable. In high-tax countries, the FTC often produces dramatically better outcomes — but once you've taken the FEIE, revocation triggers a five-year lockout. Years of suboptimal returns can mean tens of thousands in avoidable overpayment.

Often $5K–$20K+ per year, compounding

Missing the self-employment tax trap

The FEIE excludes income from income tax — but not from the 15.3% self-employment tax. Freelancers and contractors who take the FEIE and assume their tax obligation is zero are mistaken. At the 2024 exclusion limit alone, the SE tax exposure is nearly $19,000 per year.

Up to $19,330 per year at 2024 FEIE limit

Forming an LLC as a foreign owner without modeling the ECI consequences

Foreign founders default to LLCs because they seem simple. For a foreign owner, the LLC's disregarded entity status means the IRS looks through it directly — creating effectively connected income, Form 5472 filing obligations with $25,000 penalties, and treaty complications a C-Corp avoids entirely.

$25K+ Form 5472 penalty + unexpected ECI

Not planning before renouncing citizenship

The exit tax applies on the date of renunciation. Assets above the exclusion threshold ($866,000 in 2024) are treated as sold at fair market value on that date. Gifting, trust restructuring, and gain-harvesting strategies available before that date disappear the moment papers are signed.

Permanent — options close on signature date
